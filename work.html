Running social for a handbag studio

Context

House of Arella is a small studio making leather handbags that sell for $1,000 and up. The founder wanted someone to own the brand's social media so she could stop doing it herself.

What I did

In my first month I wrote the brand brief, voice guidelines, and visual identity. I went through twelve months of Instagram data in Later (17 posts and 110 stories) and used it to build a content plan around five themes: Founder, Craft, Story, Materials, and Collection. I also set up the posting schedule, caption formats, and monthly workflow.

In progress

I'm helping redesign the website and operations so we can see which content actually leads to sales, and I'm putting together a plan for Instagram and Meta ads.

Outcome

I'm the brand's only social and brand consultant. The founder now just reviews and signs off.

No. 05

Client
Sai Sankoh
Role
Brand Strategy Intern
Year
Spring 2025
Sector
Luxury Swim & Resort

E-commerce: Deciding what to put on sale

Context

Sai Sankoh is a small luxury swimwear and kaftan brand. They had more than 2,000 products and no easy way to see what was selling and what wasn't.

What I did

I ran an ABC analysis in Shopify and Excel to sort every product by how fast it moved. Then I planned promotions around the slow sellers, reorganized the stock room to match, and lined up the content calendar so posts featured the products we most needed to sell.

Outcome

About 30 to 40% of the targeted stock sold within a few weeks. I left the team a quarterly process so they can rerun the analysis on their own.

A Revolution That Wasn't

Subject

A critical review of Coperni's spring/summer 2023 collection and its spray-on dress finale. It looks at spectacle, sustainability claims, and where innovation ends and decoration begins.

Approach

The review looks at all 37 looks next to the viral finale and separates surface-level material experiments from real advances in construction. It compares the moment to McQueen's SS99 “No. 13” finale and questions how the brand talks about sustainability.

Is Gen Z Ending Outfit Repeating?

Subject

A feature story on why more college students are renting clothes, and how social pressure, subscription services, and changing ideas about ownership play into it.

Approach

The piece starts with individual students and works out to the wider market. I interviewed students at SMU and Ole Miss, plus sustainability researcher Dr. Pratyusha Kiran, and compared U.S. subscription services with Australia's more local rental culture. My conclusion: renting is a growing niche, not a replacement for owning.
